Participation model

Two signals, one consistent control set, shared by Questions and Positions. Participation is always secondary — it sharpens the content, never becomes the destination. Same widgets, same place on every card.

The two signals

Stancewhere do you land?

A two-way lean: agree ↔ disagree. On a Position it's “do you hold this view.” On a Question it's “which way do you lean” on its two implied answers.

One thing to confirm — what “stance” means on a Question. A question like “fixable, or structural?” has two implied poles, so stance = the reader's lean between them (shown above as a Fixable/Structural split). That's more useful than a generic agree/disagree. If you'd rather stance on a question mean “is this the right question / well-framed,” I'll switch it — but the two-pole lean reads cleaner and aggregates into real signal.

How each signal surfaces

Importance → order

On Questions, importance gradually reorders the list. On Positions, it sets prominence/weight but never reorders the equal-weight sides.

Stance → distribution

Agree/disagree (or the question lean) aggregates into a split bar — the reader sees where others land, not a single score.

Always secondary

Signals live in a quiet footer on each card. No counts in the hero, no leaderboards, no comments. Content first.

Design

  • One footer row on every Question and Position card: split bar + importance pill.
  • Stance tints: green on Positions; the question's blue on Questions (matches its type tag).
  • Distribution is a bar, not a number — no upvote-score vibe.
  • Controls are pills, consistent with the rest of the system.

Behavior

  • Two independent axes — stance and importance never collapse into one score.
  • Logged-out can vote (accounts deferred); de-duped lightly.
  • Hidden in Condensed / print / social — participation needs a live page.
  • Results animate in after you act, never gate reading.
