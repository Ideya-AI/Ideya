Fission-based electricity — among the lowest-carbon, lowest-death sources, in a debate that scrambles partisan lines.

Scope
Civilian fission for grid power: cost, safety, waste, the climate case. Not weapons, fusion, or naval propulsion.
Why it matters now
A retiring fleet meets climbing demand from AI and electrification, just as climate targets need firm clean power.

Questions
4 open · vote to reorder
Empirical142
Are runaway Western nuclear costs fixable, or structural?

Why contested: Korea builds at ~¼ the per-kW cost. If costs reflect lost supply chains, reform helps — if safety preferences, it won't.

Value98
Is the waste problem solved in principle, but not in practice?

Why contested: Disposal is settled science; Finland's Onkalo runs. Yet most countries have no sited repository — the gap is politics, not engineering.

Empirical64
Can new nuclear deploy fast enough to matter for 2050?

Why contested: Even optimistic build rates lag solar's growth. Critics say money decarbonizes more elsewhere; advocates say firm power is needed regardless.

The real disagreement

The debate narrows to one question.

Can the West build reactors on budget and on schedule — and is that an engineering problem or a values problem? Almost everyone agrees nuclear is low-carbon and safe.

If empirical

Overruns are contingent. Then better data could settle it.

If values

Costs encode real caution about dread risk. Then no data closes it.
