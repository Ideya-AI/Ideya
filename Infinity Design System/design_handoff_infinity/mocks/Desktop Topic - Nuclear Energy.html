Nuclear Energy

Fission-based electricity — among the lowest-carbon, lowest-death sources, in a debate that scrambles partisan lines.

Scope
Civilian fission for grid power: cost, safety, waste, the climate case. Not weapons, fusion, or naval propulsion.
Why it matters now
A retiring fleet meets climbing demand from AI and electrification, just as climate targets need firm clean power.

Empirical
Are runaway Western nuclear costs fixable, or structural?

Why it's contested: South Korea builds at ~¼ the per-kW cost of recent Western projects. If costs reflect lost supply chains, reform could restore them — if democratic safety preferences, no reform will.

Value
Is the waste problem solved in principle, but unsolved in practice?

Why it's contested: Deep geological disposal is settled science; Finland's Onkalo is operating. Yet most countries have no sited repository — the gap is political siting, not engineering.

Empirical
Can new nuclear deploy fast enough to matter for 2050?

Why it's contested: Even optimistic build rates add capacity slowly versus solar's exponential growth. Critics say the money decarbonizes more elsewhere; advocates say firm power is needed regardless.

Climate Pragmatist

“The only proven, scaled, firm low-carbon source we have.”

Nuclear is the only dispatchable zero-carbon power at scale. IPCC 1.5 °C pathways lean on expanding it; Germany's phase-out raised emissions.

Proponents: Hansen · Brand · Clean Air Task Force

Crux · where it actually sits05
The real disagreement

Strip away the rhetoric and the debate narrows to one question.

Can the West build reactors on budget and on schedule — and is that an engineering problem or a values problem? Almost everyone agrees nuclear is low-carbon and historically safe. They disagree about institutions, learning curves, and the price of caution.

If empirical

Cost overruns are contingent — supply chains, one-off designs. Then better data could settle it.

If values

High costs encode real democratic caution about dread risk. Then no data closes it — it's a choice.
