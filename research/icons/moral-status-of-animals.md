# Moral Status of Animals — primary icon art brief

Handoff brief for an illustrator or image/vector tool. Full style theme: `../ICONOGRAPHY.md`.
Template siblings: `nuclear-energy.md` (the tuned reference), `vaccine-mandates.md` and
`ai-alignment.md` (most recent), `birth-rate-decline-pronatalism.md`.
First topic in **Ethics & Philosophy** — sets the terracotta reference.

## Use
The topic's **primary hero image**. Shows large on the topic page, poster, and social
cards; also reduced into a rounded-square tinted tile and shown on a dark band. Must
read both large and small, on light and dark.

## Style (non-negotiable)
- **Minimal monoline** — ONE consistent stroke weight across the whole image, rounded
  caps/joins. References: **Gorges Beer Co** menu icons, **Flaticon "Lineal."**
- **Duotone, single color, two tones** — line at full strength + a soft (~16–18%) fill
  of the **same hue** on the masses. No second color, no gradients, no shadows.
- **Color:** the category color (Ethics & Philosophy = **ethics terracotta**,
  `#b5614a`). Deliver single-color so it can be recolored per category.
- Flat, clean, editorial.
- **Filled masses, not linework-only** — lesson from earlier topics: thin unfilled
  linework nearly vanished at 40px. **Every** major element in this scene is a filled
  shape carrying the duotone tint; nothing important may be a bare line.

## Composition
A small bespoke **scene** combining several *simple* sub-icons in one frame — about
**2.5–3.5× the complexity of a single Gorges icon**. Generate wide (3:2); framing is
normalized in post (trim to content, ~8% pad, fit 3:2 on white). Legible from ~40px
to hero size, readable on a dark background.

**A tall scene, not a low one.** The `vaccine-mandates` review flagged a composition
whose mass sat in the bottom 60% of the frame and left the top empty. This one is built
the other way: the balance scale runs the **full height** of the picture — base on the
ground line, beam near the top — and the two prints occupy the lower flanks beneath its
pans, so mass is distributed across the top, middle, and bottom and the whole thing
centers optically rather than settling on the baseline.

**Note the nearest terracotta neighbor.** No sibling topic exists yet in this category,
but the **category** hero (`categories.md` → Ethics & Philosophy) is a scale plus a head
in profile, a forking path, and a column. This scene keeps the scale and takes **none**
of the rest: no head, no profile, no spark, no forking path, no column. What separates
the two at tile size is that this scale's pans are conspicuously **empty** and it is
flanked by two prints, which are the real subject.

The subject is **the comparison itself**, not its outcome: two kinds of mark set side by
side at the same size, and an instrument for weighing that has not weighed anything.

## Elements — promise *and* peril
The debate is what grounds an animal's moral standing and how much its interests can
demand when they collide with human ones — sentience science, farming, research,
captivity, legal standing. Six camps run from humane use and human exceptionalism
through relational obligation and capabilities entitlement to equal consideration and
abolitionism. This is the hardest neutrality in the set so far, because the art can fail
in **two opposite directions**: anything that reads as animal-rights advocacy, and
anything that reads as dismissal of the question. It must do neither:
- **An upright two-pan balance scale with both pans empty ⚠︎** — the focal mass and the
  full-height element: how much an interest weighs is the topic's question, and the
  scene shows the question asked, not answered. A plain vertical post on a simple flat
  base, a straight horizontal beam across the top, two shallow pans hanging from the
  beam ends on short straight cords. The beam is **exactly level** and **both pans are
  completely empty** — nothing in them, on them, above them, or under them. Empty is the
  entire neutrality device: a **tipped** scale delivers a hierarchy (whichever way it
  tips), and a **loaded** scale held level asserts equal consideration, which is one of
  the six camps rather than the frame around them. Filled masses (both pans, the base).
- **A single animal paw print, face-on, low on one side** — echoing the topic's Phosphor
  `paw-print` glyph. One larger rounded pad with four smaller rounded toe pads in an arc
  above it, sitting on the ground line beneath one pan. Deliberately **generic** — not a
  dog, not a cat, not a hoof, not a farm species: naming a species picks the animals that
  count. **No claws** (a claw reads as threat), no fur, no leg, no ankle, and no animal
  attached to it. Filled masses (all five pads).
- **A human handprint, face-on, low on the other side, exactly the same size ⚠︎** — the
  other half of the comparison. A plain palm with five plain fingers, drawn as a flat
  stamped **print**, not a hand: no wrist, no arm, no sleeve, no fingernails, no palm
  creases. It sits on the ground line beneath the other pan at **exactly the same overall
  size and exactly the same height** as the paw print, with a clear gap between them. The
  equal size is the whole claim the picture makes — that the comparison is worth making —
  and it is the only claim it makes. The two prints **never touch, overlap, or nest**;
  a paw print set inside a human hand is a well-known advocacy motif and disqualifies the
  candidate outright. Filled mass (palm and fingers).
- **A single straight horizontal ground line across the scene** — one plane under the
  scale's base and both prints alike (mirrors the sibling compositions): the two marks
  stand on the same ground, neither raised above the other.

The neutrality is structural: two marks of identical size at identical height, a scale
that has weighed nothing, and no arrow, tether, bracket, enclosure, or connecting line
joining any of them.

## Mood
Neutral and editorial — a paw print and a handprint set out at the same size, and a
balance that has not been loaded. Whether an animal has standing, what grounds it, and
how much it can demand against a human interest are the open questions the scene holds.
Not a liberation scene, not a scene about animals as property or produce.

## Avoid
Photorealism; more than one color; gradients/shadows; **a tipped, tilted, or unbalanced
scale** in either direction, a broken, bent, or toppled scale, and **anything at all in
either pan** — a weight, a stone, a feather, an animal, a figure, a heart (verdict: the
weighing is settled, or equal consideration specifically is the answer); cages, bars,
fences, gates, pens, crates, hutches, aquariums, chains — **whole or broken** — leashes,
collars, ear tags, nets, ropes, hooks, or padlocks (verdict: captivity is the wrong, and
a *broken* chain is the loudest advocacy image there is); crossed-out symbols,
circle-and-slash marks, X marks, prohibition signs, checkmarks, or ticks over anything
(verdict: this practice is banned, or approved); a suffering, sad, caged, crying,
wounded, bleeding, thin, or cowering animal, tears, blood drops, or a body (verdict:
cruelty argument); hearts, halos, hugging, cradling, a human hand petting, stroking,
holding, reaching toward, or resting on an animal or a paw (verdict: compassion
advocacy) — and equally a hand *above* a paw, a paw *inside* a hand, or one print
overlapping or contained by the other; farm-industry signifiers of any kind — a barn,
silo, tractor, truck, feedlot, milk, eggs, wool, a cow, pig, sheep, or chicken, a joint
of meat, a bone, a plate, a fork, a knife, a price tag, a barcode, or a branding iron
(verdict: animals are produce); laboratory signifiers — a test tube, beaker, syringe,
scalpel, clipboard, cage-with-rabbit, or lab bench (verdict: research is the crime);
brains, thought bubbles, sparks, glows, auras, hearts, or light rays on the animal side
(verdict: sentience is established — which is precisely the grounds question the topic
argues about); gavels, courthouses, law scrolls, blindfolds, swords, laurels, robed
figures, temples, or classical columns (verdict: the legal-personhood camp wins, and
they also collide with the governance scene); ladders, podiums, pyramids, steps,
thrones, or anything placed higher than anything else (verdict: a ranking); a **head in
profile, a forking path, or a column** — those belong to the Ethics *category* hero and
must not be reused here; any animal, person, face, figure, hand, or arm anywhere in the
image — the two prints are the only bodies in it; globes, leaves, plants, trees,
landscapes, or an ark (verdict: environmental or salvation framing); equals signs, plus
or minus signs, question marks, arrows, chevrons, scales of percentage, gauges, or
dials; fussy internal detail (engraving or decoration on the scale, fur texture, claws,
or wrinkles on the paw pads, palm creases or fingerprint ridges on the handprint);
clutter; anything that reads as pro- or anti-animal-rights.

## Deliverable
Ideally **vector (SVG)**, single-color, transparent ground. PNG accepted
(the pipeline is raster-primary).

## Generation prompt (Nano Banana / Gemini)

frame. Lessons carried over from the earlier generations: force the soft fill to
~15–18% (it renders near-invisible if unstressed), force uniform stroke weight (thin
secondary elements drift thinner), and make every major element a **filled mass** —
earlier linework-only elements nearly vanished at 40px.

**Notes:**
- Over-generate on the **empty level balance scale ⚠︎** — it is by far the element most
  likely to come out wrong, and it fails in every direction at once. The generator wants
  to load the pans to "make it a scale," and anything it loads is a verdict: an animal in
  one pan against a person in the other, a weight on the human side, a heart on the
  animal side. It equally wants to **tip the beam**, which hands the picture to whichever
  side sits lower. And given any hint of "justice" it reaches for Lady Justice — a
  blindfold, a sword, a robed figure, a courthouse. Two empty pans on a dead-level beam,
  and nothing else, is the whole point.
- Over-generate on the **handprint reading as a print, not a hand ⚠︎**. The generator
  strongly wants to draw an actual hand — with a wrist, an arm, or reaching down toward
  the paw. A hand reaching for a paw is the single most common animal-welfare stock
  image in existence, and it delivers the compassion verdict on contact. It is a flat
  mark on the ground, the same kind of object as the paw print.
- Watch for the **paw-inside-hand motif**. A small paw nested in a large palm, a paw and
  hand touching or overlapping, or a hand cupped beneath a paw are all advocacy images
  and disqualify the candidate. So does the mirror failure of scale: a human handprint
  drawn noticeably larger than the paw print reads as human exceptionalism. Equal size,
  equal height, clear gap, no contact.
- Watch for **species creep** in the paw print. The generator will happily render a
  recognizable dog or cat paw (pets, which nobody argues about) or drift toward a hoof or
  a farm animal (which picks the industry frame). Four toe pads and one larger pad, no
  claws, no species.
- Watch for the **animal-rights vocabulary** sneaking in anywhere — a broken chain, an
  open cage, a bird flying from a cage door, a crossed-out cage, a heart over the paw.
  Any one of them ends the candidate. The mirror failure disqualifies just as fast: a
  barcode, an ear tag, a price tag, a plate, or a joint of meat.
- Watch for a **glow, spark, brain, or thought bubble** attached to the paw side. It
  looks harmless and it is not: asserting that the animal has a mind resolves the grounds
  question the topic exists to argue.
- Watch for **cross-contamination with the Ethics category hero** — a head in profile
  with a spark, a forking path, a classical column. Those belong to the category art, and
  their presence here also erodes the separation between the two terracotta tiles.
- The **sentience-science magnifying glass was considered and dropped**: a lens hovering
  over the paw would have added a fourth object, pushed the scene past the complexity
  budget, and read uncomfortably close to laboratory examination. If a full round of
  candidates fails on the scale, swapping the scale out for an unheld magnifying glass
  over the paw print is the fallback composition — but it loses the vertical fill.
- Watch for outline-only elements — every major mass must carry the terracotta fill; a
  candidate whose pans, paw pads, or handprint render as bare linework fails the 40px
  test.
- White background is intentional — the primary sits on a neutral tile; the frame
  comes from the tile, not the art.
- After generating: save as `static/img/topics/moral-status-of-animals.png`,
  set `Topic.primary_art = "img/topics/moral-status-of-animals.png"`, and
  regenerate the social PNGs
  (`render_social --slug moral-status-of-animals --formats og,sq,pt`).
