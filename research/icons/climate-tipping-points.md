# Climate Tipping Points — primary icon art brief

Handoff brief for an illustrator or image/vector tool. Full style theme: `../ICONOGRAPHY.md`.
Template siblings: `nuclear-energy.md` (the tuned reference), `content-moderation-free-speech.md`,
`ai-future-of-work.md`, `wildfires.md` (the other climate-env topic).

## Use
The topic's **primary hero image**. Shows large on the topic page, poster, and social
cards; also reduced into a rounded-square tinted tile and shown on a dark band. Must
read both large and small, on light and dark.

## Style (non-negotiable)
- **Minimal monoline** — ONE consistent stroke weight across the whole image, rounded
  caps/joins. References: **Gorges Beer Co** menu icons, **Flaticon "Lineal."**
- **Duotone, single color, two tones** — line at full strength + a soft (~16–18%) fill
  of the **same hue** on the masses. No second color, no gradients, no shadows.
- **Color:** the category color (Climate, Energy & Environment = **energy green**,
  `#4a9d6c`). Deliver single-color so it can be recolored per category.
- Flat, clean, editorial.
- **Filled masses, not linework-only** — lesson from earlier topics: thin unfilled
  curves nearly vanished at 40px. Every major element in this scene is a filled
  shape carrying the duotone tint; nothing important may be a bare line.

## Composition
A small bespoke **scene** combining several *simple* sub-icons in one frame — about
**2.5–3.5× the complexity of a single Gorges icon**. Generate wide (3:2); framing is
normalized in post (trim to content, ~8% pad, fit 3:2 on white). Legible from ~40px
to hero size, readable on a dark background.

## Elements — promise *and* peril
The debate is whether the Earth system really holds thresholds that flip abruptly and
irreversibly once crossed, and whether naming them sharpens action or muddies it. The
art must render the **threshold itself as an open question** — something poised at the
line, not yet across it — and never a verdict, neither "it has already collapsed" nor
"nothing is happening":
- **A smooth curved ridge with a small filled ball resting exactly at its crest** —
  the focal point: the canonical tipping-point figure. One simple continuous curve
  rising to a rounded summit and falling away on the other side; a small solid circle
  sitting still at the very top, perfectly balanced, centered on the apex. Not
  rolling, not part-way down either side, no motion lines, no arrow, no crack under
  it. Which way it goes — or whether it moves at all — is the topic's question.
- **A simple iceberg crossed by the waterline** — a plain angular ice mass with a
  small peak above the line and a larger mass below it, both filled: the slow part of
  the system whose scale is mostly unseen. Whole and intact — not cracked, calving,
  shrunken, or half-melted into a puddle.
- **A small six-armed snowflake floating above the scene** — echoing the topic's
  Phosphor `snowflake` glyph: six plain straight arms of the same stroke weight
  radiating from a small solid hexagonal core that carries the fill. Simply present
  in the sky, whole; not falling, not melting, not dripping.
- **A single straight horizontal line across the scene** — the waterline and horizon
  in one (mirrors the sibling compositions): the iceberg sits in it and the ridge
  rests on it, so the threshold and the system share one plane.

## Mood
Neutral and editorial — a ball held still at the top of a curve, an intact iceberg at
the waterline, a snowflake above. Whether a line exists, where it sits, and what
happens when it is crossed are the open questions the scene holds. Not a collapse
scene, not a reassurance.

## Avoid
Photorealism; more than one color; gradients/shadows; the ball shown rolling,
falling, or already down one side, and any arrow, motion trail, or dashed trajectory
showing it move (verdict: threshold crossed); a cracked, splitting, calving, or
melting-away iceberg, dripping meltwater, or a puddle (verdict: doom); a snowflake
melting or falling into water; flames, burning ground, drought cracks, dead trees,
smokestacks, storms, flooded or collapsing cities, drowning polar bears; a rising
hockey-stick chart, a red-zone dial, a thermometer, a clock at midnight, or any
warning triangle (verdict: alarm); a flat calm sea with a smiling sun or anything
that reads as reassurance; scale bars, tick marks, gauge markings, degree symbols, or
labels of any kind; fussy internal detail (ice facets, water ripples, texture on the
ridge, ornament on the snowflake); clutter; anything that reads as urgency advocacy
or as skepticism.

## Deliverable
Ideally **vector (SVG)**, single-color, transparent ground. PNG accepted
(the pipeline is raster-primary).

## Generation prompt (Nano Banana / Gemini)
**Frameless** — the art has NO drawn border. The CSS tile renders the rounded-square
frame. Lessons carried over from the earlier generations: force the soft fill to
~15–18% (it renders near-invisible if unstressed), force uniform stroke weight (thin
secondary elements drift thinner), and make every major element a **filled mass** —
earlier linework-only elements nearly vanished at 40px.

**Fresh-generation prompt:**
> A minimalist flat vector-style icon illustration of a threshold-and-balance scene, in a clean editorial line-art style. Combine several simple symbols into one small, balanced composition: on one side, a smooth simple curved ridge — a single continuous curve that rises to a rounded summit and slopes away on the other side — with one small solid filled circle resting perfectly still exactly at the very top of the summit, centered on the apex and perfectly balanced, NOT rolling and NOT part-way down either slope, with no arrow, no motion lines, and no dashed path; on the other side, a simple angular iceberg — a plain ice mass with a small peak above the water and a larger mass below the water, both parts drawn as solid filled shapes, whole and intact with no cracks and no drips; and above the scene, one small simple six-armed snowflake — six plain straight arms radiating evenly from a small solid hexagonal center — floating whole in the sky. A single thin, straight horizontal line runs across the whole scene as both the waterline and the horizon — the iceberg sits in this line with its larger mass below it, and the curved ridge rests on this same line — no landscape, no coastline, no waves.
>
> Important: NO border, NO frame, NO box or outline around the scene — just the illustration itself on a plain background. Keep the composition compact and centered, not spread wide, with the ridge-and-ball and the iceberg at roughly equal visual weight on either side of the center and the small snowflake clearly smaller than both.
>
> Style: monoline line art with ONE uniform stroke weight on every element — the curved ridge, the small ball, the iceberg above and below the water, all six snowflake arms, and the horizontal waterline must all be exactly the same thickness. Rounded line caps and corners. Confident, slightly bold strokes so it stays legible when small, about 2.5 to 3.5 times the complexity of one simple glyph — each element stays simple, the richness is the combination. No fussy internal detail — no facets or shading lines on the iceberg, no ripples in the water, no texture on the ridge, no ornament or side-branches on the snowflake arms.
>
> Color: a single green hue, two tones only. Draw all lines in solid green (#4a9d6c), and give EVERY major element a clearly visible soft fill of the same green at about 16-18% opacity — fill the body of the curved ridge below its curve, the small ball, both the above-water and below-water parts of the iceberg, and the snowflake's hexagonal center with a light green tint so the two-tone duotone is obviously present rather than white interiors; no major element may be left as unfilled outline-only linework. No other colors, no gradients, no shadows, no offset or misregistered fills, no 3D, no texture.
>
> Composition: wide 3:2 aspect, centered with generous, even padding; balanced optical weight — the waterline runs level through the middle, the ridge with its poised ball anchors one side, the iceberg the other, the snowflake sits small above. Plain white background. Calm, neutral, editorial mood — something resting exactly at a threshold with the outcome left open; not a disaster scene, not a reassurance. No cracked or melting ice, no meltwater drips or puddles, no flames, no storms, no dead trees, no smokestacks, no cities, no animals, no charts or graph lines, no thermometers, no dials or gauges, no clocks, no warning triangles, no arrows. No text, letters, words, numbers, tick marks, or scale markings anywhere in the image.

**Edit-mode instruction (after the first generation, feed the concept back in):**
> Using this image, keep the same composition, elements, and layout, but: (1) remove any border or frame around the scene entirely; (2) add a clearly visible soft light-green fill (~16% opacity of the same hue) inside EVERY major element — the body of the curved ridge, the small ball, both the above-water and below-water parts of the iceberg, and the snowflake's hexagonal center — so it reads as a two-tone duotone with no outline-only elements left; (3) make every line exactly the same thickness, matching the main outline weight — including the snowflake's six arms, the ridge curve, and the horizontal waterline; (4) keep the ball resting exactly at the very top of the ridge, still and centered on the apex, with no arrow, motion lines, or dashed path, and keep the iceberg whole and uncracked with no drips. Keep it flat, single green hue, no gradients, shadows, extra colors, or text.

**Notes:**
- Over-generate on the **ball at the apex** and the **snowflake**; those are the
  elements most likely to come out wrong — the generator tends to nudge the ball down
  one slope, merge it into the ridge curve, or add an arrow, and it renders snowflake
  arms thinner than the main stroke or crusts them with fussy side-branches.
- Watch for the generator editorializing — a crack across the iceberg, meltwater
  drips, a ball tipping or already rolling downhill, a motion arrow, or a rising chart
  line sneak in easily on this topic; any of those breaks the required neutrality and
  disqualifies the candidate. The mirror failure also disqualifies: a scene so placid
  it reads as "nothing to see here."
- Watch for the below-water part of the iceberg being dropped or left as bare
  linework — the submerged mass must be present and filled.
- Watch for outline-only elements — every major mass must carry the green fill; a
  candidate whose ridge or iceberg renders as bare linework fails the 40px test.
- White background is intentional — the primary sits on a neutral tile; the frame
  comes from the tile, not the art.
- After generating: save as `static/img/topics/climate-tipping-points.png`,
  set `Topic.primary_art = "img/topics/climate-tipping-points.png"`, and
  regenerate the social PNGs
  (`render_social --slug climate-tipping-points --formats og,sq,pt`).
