# AI Alignment Approaches — primary icon art brief

Handoff brief for an illustrator or image/vector tool. Full style theme: `../ICONOGRAPHY.md`.
Template siblings: `nuclear-energy.md` (the tuned reference),
`frontier-ai-regulation.md` (most recent), `ai-takeoff-loss-of-control.md`
and `ai-future-of-work.md` (the other AI-category scenes — this one must not echo them).

## Use
The topic's **primary hero image**. Shows large on the topic page, poster, and social
cards; also reduced into a rounded-square tinted tile and shown on a dark band. Must
read both large and small, on light and dark.

## Style (non-negotiable)
- **Minimal monoline** — ONE consistent stroke weight across the whole image, rounded
  caps/joins. References: **Gorges Beer Co** menu icons, **Flaticon "Lineal."**
- **Duotone, single color, two tones** — line at full strength + a soft (~16–18%) fill
  of the **same hue** on the masses. No second color, no gradients, no shadows.
- **Color:** the category color (AI & Machine Intelligence = **tech blue**,
  `#3b7ea1`). Deliver single-color so it can be recolored per category.
- Flat, clean, editorial.
- **Filled masses, not linework-only** — lesson from earlier topics: thin unfilled
  linework nearly vanished at 40px. **Every** major element in this scene is a filled
  shape carrying the duotone tint; nothing important may be a bare line.

## Composition
A small bespoke **scene** combining several *simple* sub-icons in one frame — about
**2.5–3.5× the complexity of a single Gorges icon**. Generate wide (3:2); framing is
normalized in post (trim to content, ~8% pad, fit 3:2 on white). Legible from ~40px
to hero size, readable on a dark background.

**Distinct from the other AI-category scenes.** `ai-takeoff-loss-of-control` is a
trajectory scene (two rising curves, a rocket, a figure at a lever); `ai-future-of-work`
is a two-party scene (a shared worktable, a person on one side, a machine arm on the
other); `frontier-ai-regulation` is a **low, horizontal** still life (a gavel lying flat,
a page lying flat, a chip). This one is a **bench of instruments** — three upright
measuring and inspecting tools standing side by side, no human figure and no machine
figure, with a tall circular focal mass and one diagonal line (the arrow) breaking the
verticals. Its silhouette is upright and round where the regulation scene's is flat and
low, which is what keeps the two apart in the same blue at tile size.

The subject is the **contest of methods**, not a result: several different instruments
for aiming a system at an intended goal, standing together on one level plane, none of
them marked as the one that works.

## Elements — promise *and* peril
The debate is which technical program actually makes an advanced system pursue its
intended goal — training on human feedback, auditing what is going on inside the model,
containment and control, amplified oversight, formal proof — and whether any of it can
be **verified**. The art must show the methods side by side with the verdict withheld;
it must not say alignment is solved and must not say it is doomed:
- **An upright round target with a single arrow struck slightly off the center** —
  the focal point, echoing the topic's Phosphor `target` glyph: intended goal versus
  actual behavior. A plain face-on target of two or three concentric rings with a small
  center dot, standing on the ground line on a simple short stand. One arrow — plain
  shaft with a simple head and two straight flights — is embedded in the target
  **inside the innermost ring, adjacent to but not touching the center dot**. The near
  miss is the honest image and the whole neutrality of the piece: the shot landed, it is
  close, and it is not on the mark. **Not a dead-center bullseye** (verdict: alignment
  achieved) and **not a wide miss, a shot in the outer edge, a stray arrow in the ground,
  a snapped shaft, or a knocked-over target** (verdict: alignment fails). Exactly one
  arrow. Filled mass.
- **A magnifying glass held over a small node graph, unheld ⚠︎** — the interpretability
  audit: reading a model's internals rather than only its outputs. A plain round lens
  with a short straight handle, angled over a small cluster of four or five connected
  dots that sits on the ground line beneath it. The glass **hovers by itself — no hand,
  no arm, no figure anywhere in the image**. The node graph underneath is plain and
  intact: no face, no eye, no brain shape, no glow, no crack, no spark, and nothing
  enclosing it. The lens shows the nodes plainly; it neither magnifies a flaw nor
  reveals a hidden creature. Filled masses (lens body and nodes).
- **A pair of plain nested frames standing empty ⚠︎** — containment and control as one
  method among several: a boundary drawn, with the question of what it holds left open.
  Two simple concentric rounded-square outlines, one inside the other with an even gap,
  standing upright on the same ground line. The inner frame is **completely empty** —
  nothing inside it, nothing trapped, nothing being held back: **no chip, no creature,
  no robot, no figure, no bars, no lock, no chain, no mesh, and no crossbars**. Filled
  masses (the band between the two frames carries the tint; the inner opening stays
  clear).
- **A single straight horizontal ground line across the scene** — one plane under the
  target's stand, the node graph, and the frames alike (mirrors the sibling
  compositions): the methods stand level with one another, none raised above the rest.

The neutrality is structural: three instruments of comparable visual weight on one
level line, an arrow that neither hits nor misses, and no mark, tick, cross, score, or
connecting line saying which instrument is the right one.

## Mood
Neutral and editorial — a shot that landed near the mark, a lens over a model's
internals, a boundary drawn around nothing yet, all standing on the same plane. Which
program works, and whether anyone can check that it worked, are the open questions the
scene holds. Not a safety-achieved scene, not a doom scene, not a warning.

## Avoid
Photorealism; more than one color; gradients/shadows; **an arrow in the dead center of
the target**, a bullseye split by a second arrow, a cluster of arrows in the gold, a
checkmark, a tick, a thumbs-up, a laurel, a halo, or a star (verdict: alignment
achieved); an arrow missing the target entirely, stuck in the ground, bouncing off,
snapped, bent, or falling, a toppled or cracked target, arrows aimed at anything other
than the target, and any second or third arrow anywhere (verdict: alignment fails);
warning triangles, exclamation marks, hazard stripes, alarm marks, skulls, mushroom
clouds, a crack, spark, flame, or glow on the node graph, a red zone or danger dial
(verdict: AI is dangerous); shields, padlocks, chains, cages, bars, nets, fences,
leashes, tethers, or a closed lid — and anything at all placed inside the nested frames
(verdict: safety secured, or AI must be shackled); crossed-out symbols, X marks, error
marks, or a broken instrument (verdict: the method has failed); anthropomorphic or
humanoid robots, robot heads, robot hands, glowing brains, a single watching eye, or a
face on any element; any human figure, hand, arm, or silhouette at all — including a
hand holding the magnifying glass or drawing the bow; a bow, bowstring, quiver, dartboard
darts, or any launching apparatus (the arrow is already at rest in the target); gauges,
dials, needles, progress bars, scoreboards, percentages, star ratings, thermometers,
countdown clocks, or hourglasses; balance scales, gavels, blank pages, or pens (those
belong to `frontier-ai-regulation` and must not be reused here); trajectory curves,
rockets, worktables, or machine arms (those belong to the other two AI scenes); arrows
used as pointers, connectors, chevrons, or motion trails anywhere in the scene — the
single arrow in the target is the only arrow in the image; national flags, seals, or
maps; fussy internal detail (circuit traces or a die pattern in the node graph, ring
numbering or scoring marks on the target, feather barbs on the flights, a screw or
engraving on the magnifier); clutter; anything that reads as reassurance or as alarm.

## Deliverable
Ideally **vector (SVG)**, single-color, transparent ground. PNG accepted
(the pipeline is raster-primary).

## Generation prompt (Nano Banana / Gemini)
**Frameless** — the art has NO drawn border. The CSS tile renders the rounded-square
frame. Lessons carried over from the earlier generations: force the soft fill to
~15–18% (it renders near-invisible if unstressed), force uniform stroke weight (thin
secondary elements drift thinner), and make every major element a **filled mass** —
earlier linework-only elements nearly vanished at 40px.

**Fresh-generation prompt:**
> A minimalist flat vector-style icon illustration of a scene of measuring instruments, in a clean editorial line-art style. Combine several simple objects into one small, balanced composition, all of them standing on a single thin straight horizontal ground line that runs across the whole scene: at the center, an upright round archery target seen face-on — two or three plain concentric rings with a small round dot at the middle — resting on a simple short stand, with exactly ONE simple arrow struck into it, the arrow shaft plain with a simple pointed head and two straight flights, and the arrow embedded INSIDE the innermost ring right beside the center dot but clearly NOT in the center dot itself — a very near miss, not a bullseye and not a wide miss, with no other arrows anywhere; on one side, a simple magnifying glass — a plain round lens with a short straight handle — hovering by itself at an angle above a small plain cluster of four or five dots joined by short straight links that sits on the ground line beneath it, with NO hand, NO arm and NO person holding the magnifying glass; and on the other side, two plain concentric rounded-square frames standing upright on the same ground line, one inside the other with an even gap between them, and the inner frame COMPLETELY EMPTY — nothing inside it at all, no chip, no creature, no robot, no figure, no bars, no crossbars, no lock and no mesh. There is NO person, NO hand, NO robot and NO face anywhere in the image, and nothing connects the three objects — no arrow pointer, no line, no rope, no chain, no bracket and no enclosure around any of them.
>
> Important: NO border, NO frame, NO box or outline around the whole scene — the two small nested rounded-square frames are one object standing on the ground line inside the picture, not a border around the picture. Keep the composition compact and centered, not spread wide, with the target, the magnifying glass over the dots, and the nested frames at roughly EQUAL visual weight and standing at the same level on the one ground line — none of them larger, taller, higher, or more dominant than the others, and none placed above the others.
>
> Style: monoline line art with ONE uniform stroke weight on every element — the target rings and its stand, the arrow shaft, head and flights, the magnifying glass lens and handle, the dots and the short links between them, both nested frames, and the ground line must all be exactly the same thickness. Rounded line caps and corners. Confident, slightly bold strokes so it stays legible when small, about 2.5 to 3.5 times the complexity of one simple glyph — each object stays simple, the richness is the combination. No fussy internal detail — no numbers or scoring marks on the target rings, no feather barbs on the arrow flights, no screw or engraving on the magnifying glass, no circuit traces or inner pattern in the cluster of dots.
>
> Color: a single blue hue, two tones only. Draw all lines in solid blue (#3b7ea1), and give EVERY major element a clearly visible soft fill of the same blue at about 16-18% opacity — fill the target's rings and its stand, the arrow shaft and head, the magnifying glass lens, the round dots of the cluster, and the band between the two nested frames with a light blue tint so the two-tone duotone is obviously present rather than white interiors; no major element may be left as unfilled outline-only linework. No other colors, no gradients, no shadows, no offset or misregistered fills, no 3D, no texture.
>
> Composition: wide 3:2 aspect, centered with generous, even padding; balanced optical weight — the level ground line runs along the bottom, the round target anchors the middle, the magnifying glass and the dots on one side, the empty nested frames on the other. Plain white background. Calm, neutral, editorial mood — several instruments for aiming and checking a system standing side by side, with which one works left an open question; not a success scene, not a warning, not a danger scene. No bullseye hit, no second arrow, no missed or fallen arrow, no toppled or cracked target, no checkmarks, ticks, crosses or X marks, no warning triangles, no hazard signs, no skulls, no shields, no padlocks, no chains, no cages, no bars or nets, no leashes, no robots, no brains, no eyes, no faces, no human figures or hands, no bows or quivers, no gauges, dials, needles, progress bars or clocks, no rockets, no curves or charts, no flags, no globes, no maps. No text, letters, words, numbers, tick marks, percent signs or scale markings anywhere in the image, including on the target rings.

**Edit-mode instruction (after the first generation, feed the concept back in):**
> Using this image, keep the same composition, elements, and layout, but: (1) remove any border or frame around the whole scene entirely, keeping only the small pair of nested rounded-square frames standing on the ground line as an object inside the picture; (2) add a clearly visible soft light-blue fill (~16% opacity of the same hue) inside EVERY major element — the target rings and stand, the arrow shaft and head, the magnifying glass lens, the dots of the cluster, and the band between the two nested frames — so it reads as a two-tone duotone with no outline-only elements left; (3) make every line exactly the same thickness, matching the main outline weight — including the links between the dots, the magnifying glass handle, the arrow flights, and the ground line; (4) place exactly ONE arrow in the target, struck inside the innermost ring right beside the center dot but not in it, and delete any additional arrows, any arrow lying on the ground or missing the target, and any bow, bowstring or quiver; (5) keep the inner nested frame completely empty — remove anything drawn inside it, and remove any bars, crossbars, lock, chain or mesh; (6) keep the small cluster of dots plain and whole — remove any crack, glow, spark, face, eye or inner pattern — and remove any hand, arm or figure holding the magnifying glass; (7) keep the target, the magnifying glass with its dots, and the nested frames at equal size and equal height on the same ground line, none above the others; (8) remove any checkmark, tick, cross, X mark, warning triangle, shield or padlock. Keep it flat, single blue hue, no gradients, shadows, extra colors, or text.

**Notes:**
- Over-generate on the **near-miss arrow**, the **unheld magnifying glass**, and the
  **empty nested frames** — those are the three elements most likely to come out wrong.
  The generator badly wants to plant the arrow dead center in the bullseye (verdict:
  alignment solved) or, given any hint of tension, to throw it wide, break it, or knock
  the target over (verdict: alignment fails); either one answers the question the scene
  exists to hold open. It equally wants to attach a hand to the magnifying glass — a
  hand puts a person in charge of the audit, which this scene does not claim — and to
  fill the inner nested frame with a chip, a robot, or a caged shape, which turns
  containment from a method into the verdict "AI must be locked up."
- Watch for the nested frames being read as a **border around the whole picture**
  instead of an object in it. If the result comes back with the scene boxed in, that is
  a frame violation, not the containment element; re-roll or fix it in edit mode.
- Watch for the generator editorializing in either direction — a checkmark, a shield, or
  a halo over the target (verdict: alignment is working), or a warning triangle, a
  cracked or sparking node graph, a padlock, a chain, or a menacing robot (verdict:
  doom). Both mirror failures disqualify the candidate.
- Watch for a second arrow. The generator often adds two or three arrows to "make it a
  target"; a spread of arrows reads as a scoring result and a lone arrow near the middle
  is the entire point.
- Watch for cross-contamination with the sibling AI scenes — a gavel, blank page, or pen
  (`frontier-ai-regulation`), a rising curve or rocket (`ai-takeoff-loss-of-control`), a
  worktable or machine arm (`ai-future-of-work`). None of those belong here, and their
  presence also erodes the visual separation between four blue tiles.
- Watch for outline-only elements — every major mass must carry the blue fill; a
  candidate whose target rings, lens, or dots render as bare linework fails the 40px
  test.
- White background is intentional — the primary sits on a neutral tile; the frame
  comes from the tile, not the art.
- After generating: save as `static/img/topics/ai-alignment.png`,
  set `Topic.primary_art = "img/topics/ai-alignment.png"`, and
  regenerate the social PNGs
  (`render_social --slug ai-alignment --formats og,sq,pt`).

## Selection

Winner: `candidates/ai-alignment/ai-alignment.gemini-3-pro-image.3.jpg`, first
generation round. All four candidates hit the bench composition; #2 was disqualified
for fills far heavier than the family's light tints, #1 for a stray vertical line
artifact beside the nested frames, #4 for an outline-only magnifier lens (the
filled-masses rule). #3 fills every mass at the right weight, keeps one stroke
width, and the arrow strikes at the center dot's edge rather than dead-center — the
closest to the brief's near-miss. 40px thumb verified legible.
