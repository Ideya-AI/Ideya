## Notes for the human
- Stat "Premature deaths from fossil fuel air pollution": the glance note says the
  study's "own range runs from below zero to 14 million". A reader meeting
  "below zero" premature deaths has nothing in the tile that explains it, and the
  detail does not return to it. The point (the interval crosses zero, so the
  estimate is not robust) would land with a half-clause naming it a confidence
  interval.
- The European Parliament's Santa Marta briefing is the named source for four
  separate figures, in questions 3, 4 and 5 and in position 5. Nothing is wrong
  with it, but a reader notices the same secondary document carrying the
  Ukraine price moves, the methane share, the Global South reserve share and the
  solar cost curve.
- The "build the replacement first" argument is met three times: as the second
  lean in question 2, as the closing move of position 2, and as the whole of
  position 5. Each framing differs, so nothing is restated, but it is the most
  repeated idea on the page.
- Source lines mix data year and publication year: "Global Energy Monitor, Boom
  and Bust Coal 2026 · 2025" and "OECD Inventory of Support Measures for Fossil
  Fuels 2025 · 2024" each pair a title year with an earlier stamp. Internally
  consistent as a data-year convention, mildly odd to read.
- Actions map two items to position 1 (the BOGA declaration and the subsidy
  coalition) and one each to positions 2 through 5. The subsidy coalition is
  arguably a page-level resource rather than a position-1 one.
- Three Basics tiles make the same point with three different measures: planned
  2030 output against a 1.5 °C path, emissions committed by built extraction, and
  the share of reserves that must stay down. They are genuinely different
  quantities with different policy implications, but the cumulative effect in the
  section is one message stated three ways.
- Position 5 carries three proponents against five or six for the others; it reads
  as the thinnest-backed camp even though its argument is the longest.
