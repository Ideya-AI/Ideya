## Notes for the human
- Repeated argument: the point that data about one person reveals others appears in question 2's detail, question 6's why, the Ban detail, the Ownership rebuttal and the crux values. The point that privacy rules entrench the biggest firms (the GDPR vendor-concentration finding) appears in the GDPR stat detail, question 3, question 5, the Protect rebuttal, the Light-standard detail and the Competition detail. A reader meets each point five or six times. None of these is a verbatim repeat, and each is a fair place for the point, but a trim would tighten the page.
- Question 3, "adds little" side: "The largest estimate comes from a company that sells tracking-based advertising". The chart just above shows Johnson et al. (independent) at the same 52 percent as Google's test, so the largest figure is a tie. A reader may notice the camp passing over the independent study. This is camp voice and changes nothing, but "One of the two largest estimates" would be exact.
- Ad market share tile: the detail says openly that WPP's release does not define the market and that "outside China" is the page's own reading. The Competition position then states it as fact ("57.6 percent of the world’s advertising market outside China"). Worth confirming against the WPP Media midyear release which market the share is of.
- Pew tile detail: it gives 2019-survey figures (81 percent say risks outweigh benefits, 72 percent benefit little) under a link to the 2023 report. That is fine if the 2023 report restates them. Otherwise the 2019 report should be named. Pew's October 2023 report would settle it.
- US states tile: "Alabama, Louisiana, Oklahoma and Vermont were the 2026 additions" and the total of 24. My memory is unsure whether Oklahoma's law was enacted in 2025. The Byte Back post of June 29, 2026 would settle it. No conclusion changes either way.
- Publisher revenue tile: only the Google row is linked. The other four rows are named by author, venue and year in the detail, which a reader can follow by search. This is acceptable under the rule, but it is the thinnest sourcing on the page.
- The "Extra clicks" tile shows a bare "40%" headline with a bar filled to 40 percent. The figure is an "up to" maximum and an increase, not a share. The note says "up to", but the bar visual reads as a proportion. This is the bar viz template applied to a percent-change value, and the same applies to the 12 percent GDPR tile.
- Actions: Privacy Badger (an EFF tool) is tagged to the Ban camp, while EFF is listed as a proponent of the Competition camp. EFF has also called for banning behavioral ads, so this is defensible, but a reader may find the tag odd. The Protect camp's one tagged action is the California deletion tool rather than a statement of its case. Every other camp in the first six actions gets a "read the case for" link.
- Title: "Surveillance Capitalism" is Shoshana Zuboff's critical coinage, while the slug and scope speak of data ownership. It is the common name of the debate, and the page text stays neutral. It is an index field, so it is your call.
- Layout (from views.md, content-caused, minor): "Consent can’t work" wraps to two lines at 390. At 1440, "17 billion" pushes the unit "signals" to its own line in the narrow tile. Bar labels "Concerned about use", "FTC v. Facebook, 2019" and "Texas v. Google, 2025" wrap at 1440. None of these misleads.
- Ranked charts show no unit on the bars ($ billion, %, % less, $ a year), so a reader learns the unit only from the note. This is a template behavior, not specific to this topic.
