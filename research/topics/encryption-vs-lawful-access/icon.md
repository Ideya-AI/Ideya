# Encryption vs. Lawful Access — primary icon art brief

Handoff brief for an illustrator or image/vector tool. Full style theme: `../ICONOGRAPHY.md`.
Template siblings: `nuclear-energy.md` (the tuned reference), `moral-status-of-animals.md`,
`vaccine-mandates.md` and `ai-alignment.md` (most recent),
`content-moderation-free-speech.md` (the other **Digital Society** scene — this one must
not echo it).

## Use
The topic's **primary hero image**. Shows large on the topic page, poster, and social
cards; also reduced into a rounded-square tinted tile and shown on a dark band. Must
read both large and small, on light and dark.

## Style (non-negotiable)
- **Minimal monoline** — ONE consistent stroke weight across the whole image, rounded
  caps/joins. References: **Gorges Beer Co** menu icons, **Flaticon "Lineal."**
- **Duotone, single color, two tones** — line at full strength + a soft (~16–18%) fill
  of the **same hue** on the masses. No second color, no gradients, no shadows.
- **Color:** the category color (Digital Society & Information = **digital teal**,
  `#2f8f86`). Deliver single-color so it can be recolored per category.
- Flat, clean, editorial.
- **Filled masses, not linework-only** — lesson from earlier topics: thin unfilled
  linework nearly vanished at 40px. **Every** major element in this scene is a filled
  shape carrying the duotone tint; nothing important may be a bare line.

## Composition
A small bespoke **scene** combining several *simple* sub-icons in one frame — about
**2.5–3.5× the complexity of a single Gorges icon**. Generate wide (3:2); framing is
normalized in post (trim to content, ~8% pad, fit 3:2 on white). Legible from ~40px
to hero size, readable on a dark background.

An **object-only still life** on one ground line — no figure, no hand, no arm, no face.
Four objects: the thing that is locked, the lock itself, the key that is not in it, and
the legal instrument that asks for it. Its silhouette is a **tall rounded mass flanked by
two upright rectangles with one low horizontal accent between them**, which is what
separates it at tile size from `content-moderation-free-speech`'s post-and-lintel gateway
with bubbles flying through it. That scene is about traffic passing a gate; this one has
no traffic and no gate — nothing in it moves.

**A tall scene, not a low one.** The padlock runs most of the picture's height, shackle
near the top, so mass is distributed across the top, middle and bottom rather than
settling along the baseline.

**Note the nearest teal neighbors.** The **category** hero (`categories.md` → Digital
Society) is a globe with network lines, a chat bubble, a small padlock and a screen. This
scene keeps a padlock — unavoidable for the topic, and it is the topic's Phosphor glyph —
and takes **none** of the rest: no globe, no network lines or dots, no chat or speech
bubble, no screen, no phone. What separates it from the category tile is that here the
padlock is the large focal mass rather than a small accessory, and it is accompanied by a
loose key, a sealed envelope and a sealed page instead of connectivity motifs.

The subject is **whether the key should exist at all**, not whether it should be turned:
a lock that is closed, a key lying near it that has never been in it, a message still
sealed, and a warrant standing at rest. Nothing in the frame acts on anything else.

## Elements — promise *and* peril
The debate is whether governments may compel access to end-to-end encrypted
communications — exceptional-access mandates, client-side scanning on the device, or
targeted hacking of endpoints — weighed against the fact that the same encryption secures
everyone's banking, medicine, journalism and dissent. Camps run from law-enforcement
access mandates and device-side scanning through targeted-lawful-hacking compromises to
security absolutism. The art must hold the *question* open — it must not celebrate
unbreakable encryption and must not depict the state as a menace:
- **A closed padlock standing upright on the ground line ⚠︎** — the focal mass and the
  full-height element, echoing the topic's Phosphor `lock-key` glyph. A plain rounded
  rectangular body with a simple semicircular shackle arching from its top, **both ends of
  the shackle seated in the body so the lock is unmistakably closed**, and one small plain
  keyhole centered on the body — a round hole with a short straight slot below it. The
  lock is **whole, ordinary and undramatic**: not oversized armor, not a vault door, not
  reinforced, not a safe, and equally not cracked, dented, sprung, sawn, pried, snapped,
  or hanging open. **The keyhole is a keyhole, never an eye** — no pupil, no iris, no lash.
  A fortified lock renders "encryption is inviolable"; a broken one renders "access wins."
  Filled mass (body and shackle).
- **A single plain key lying flat on the ground line beside the padlock, not in it ⚠︎** —
  the exceptional-access question stated as an object: whether a key should exist at all,
  drawn as one that exists and is in nothing. Seen side-on and level: a plain round bow
  (ring) at one end, a straight shaft, a short simple bit at the other end. It lies on the
  same ground line with **a clear empty gap between it and the padlock — never inserted in
  the keyhole, never touching the lock, never hanging from the shackle, never on a ring,
  chain, cord, or lanyard, and never held**. Its bit **points away from the padlock**, not
  toward the keyhole; a key aimed at the lock reads as about to turn. Exactly one key —
  never a bunch, never a duplicate pair, never broken, bent, snapped, or crossed out.
  Filled mass.
- **A sealed envelope standing upright on the ground line ⚠︎** — what is at stake: the
  private message, drawn at rest and intact. A plain rectangle standing on its long edge
  with a shallow triangular flap drawn across its upper portion, **closed and unopened**:
  no letter pulled out or peeking from it, no torn edge, no slit, no opened flap, no
  contents visible, no stamp, no address, no writing, and **no padlock, chain, shield, or
  seal drawn onto it** — a locked-up envelope renders the security verdict. It stands
  alone with a clear gap and touches nothing. Filled mass.
- **An upright rectangular page with one small circular seal near its lower edge** — the
  warrant: lawful authority present in the scene as a document at rest rather than as
  force. A plain portrait sheet standing on the ground line, **blank except for ONE small
  plain circle** low on it standing for a seal — no words, no lines of text, no heading,
  no signature, no ribbon, no crest, no eagle, no star, and no emblem inside the circle.
  It is not torn, crumpled, stamped "denied," crossed out, or on fire, and it does not
  overlap, lean on, or point at the padlock. The instrument is valid and it is simply
  standing there; whether it can be executed against the lock is the whole topic and the
  scene declines to say. Filled mass.
- **A single straight horizontal ground line across the scene** — one plane under the
  page, the padlock, the key and the envelope alike (mirrors the sibling compositions):
  the legal instrument and the locked message stand on the same ground, neither above the
  other.

The neutrality is structural: a lock nobody is opening, a key in nothing, a message still
sealed, a warrant nobody is serving, and no arrow, tether, bracket, enclosure, or
connecting line joining any of them.

## Mood
Neutral and editorial — a closed lock, a loose key, an unopened envelope, a sealed page,
all set out on one plane. Whether a key should exist, who could hold it, and what its
existence costs everyone else are the open questions the scene holds. Not a
privacy-triumph scene, not a surveillance-threat scene.

## Avoid
Photorealism; more than one color; gradients/shadows; **an eye of any kind** — a watching
eye, an eye in the keyhole, an eye in a triangle, a pupil, an eye above or behind the
envelope (verdict: surveillance menace, and the loudest cliché on this topic); security
cameras, CCTV domes, satellite dishes, antennas, drones, radar arcs, listening bugs,
microphones, ears, headphones, wiretaps, or a hooded or masked figure (verdict:
surveillance menace); magnifying glasses, binoculars, telescopes, or scanning beams over
the envelope (verdict: the message is being read — and the magnifier belongs to
`ai-alignment`); a hand, glove, arm, crowbar, hammer, chisel, pick, drill, saw, or tool
touching, holding, or working on the lock, a key inserted or turning in the keyhole, a
sprung, snapped, cracked, cut, dented, or fallen padlock, a broken or bent key, a literal
door or hatch drawn in the lock body as a "backdoor" (verdict: access achieved, or
encryption is defeated); **an oversized, armored, reinforced, glowing, or shielded
padlock**, a bank vault, safe, strongbox, steel wall, fortress, castle, dome, force
field, shield, halo, or aura around the envelope or the lock, a padlock with no keyhole,
a chain wrapping the envelope, or a key crossed out, in a prohibition circle, snapped in
half, or thrown away (verdict: no key may exist — encryption wins); checkmarks, ticks,
X marks, crosses, prohibition or circle-and-slash marks, error marks, warning triangles,
exclamation marks, hazard stripes, alarm bells, red zones, or skulls (verdict rendered,
either direction); gavels, courthouses, capitol domes, columns, flags, badges, uniforms,
official caps, national seals, crests, eagles, stars, or a police shield (verdict: the
state overpowers the person — and columns and gavels belong to the governance and ethics
category heroes); balance scales, gateways with posts and a lintel, and speech or chat
bubbles (those belong to `content-moderation-free-speech` and must not be reused here);
globes, network node graphs, connection lines, screens, monitors, laptops, phones,
smartphones, servers, clouds, wifi arcs, or signal waves (those belong to the Digital
category hero, and a phone with anything on it renders the device-scanning verdict);
binary digits, 1s and 0s, hexadecimal, code, ciphertext, scrambled characters, QR codes,
barcodes, or fingerprints (text is forbidden outright, and a fingerprint renders identity
unlocked); any human figure, hand, arm, face, or silhouette at all; fussy internal detail
(engraving, rivets, brand marks, a dial, a combination wheel, or a shine highlight on the
padlock; teeth-by-teeth serration or a filigree bow on the key; a lining pattern, address
lines, stamp, or postmark on the envelope; ruled lines, margins, or an emblem on the
page); clutter; anything that reads as pro-encryption or pro-access.

## Deliverable
Ideally **vector (SVG)**, single-color, transparent ground. PNG accepted
(the pipeline is raster-primary).

**Notes:**
- Over-generate on the **key lying loose ⚠︎** — it is by far the element most likely to
  come out wrong. The generator wants a key to be *in* something: it will slide it into
  the keyhole, stand it against the lock, hook it through the shackle, or hang it on a
  ring. Any of those turns the scene into "the lock is being opened," which is exactly the
  verdict the topic exists to argue about. The mirror failure is just as fatal: given any
  hint of tension it snaps the key, bends it, or slashes it out — that renders "no key may
  exist," which is the security-absolutist camp, not the frame around the camps. One whole
  key, lying flat on the ground, bit pointed away, with air between it and the lock.
- Over-generate on the **padlock staying an ordinary closed padlock ⚠︎**. It fails in both
  directions and it is the focal mass, so a failure there sinks the candidate. The
  generator inflates it into armor — a vault door, a steel wall, a glow, a shield behind
  the envelope — which renders "encryption is inviolable"; or it cracks, dents, saws, or
  springs it open, which renders "access wins." Plain, shut, whole, undramatic.
- Watch for the **keyhole turning into an eye**. It is the single most common
  encryption-debate illustration in existence, and one pupil in that hole flips the whole
  piece to "surveillance menace." Same for an eye hovering over the envelope. A round hole
  with a short slot, nothing else.
- Watch for the **envelope being editorialized** — a padlock stamped on it, a chain around
  it, or a shield behind it (verdict: encryption protects you), versus a torn edge, a slit,
  a letter being drawn out, or a magnifier over it (verdict: your mail is being read).
  Sealed, plain, and untouched is the whole point.
- Watch for the **warrant page acquiring authority theater** — an eagle, a crest, a star,
  a ribbon on the seal, a gavel beside it, a flag behind it, or a courthouse. The circle
  is a seal only in the sense that it is a circle; naming a state makes the picture a
  claim about that state's power, and columns and gavels also collide with the governance
  and ethics category heroes.
- Watch for **crypto decoration** — 1s and 0s, hex, scrambled glyphs, a data stream, a
  shield-with-lock badge, a QR code. Text of any kind disqualifies the candidate outright,
  and this topic attracts it more than any other in the set.
- Watch for cross-contamination with `content-moderation-free-speech`, the other teal
  tile — a post-and-lintel gateway, a level balance scale, or speech bubbles with three
  dots. None of those belong here, and their presence also erodes the separation between
  the two teal tiles. Same for the Digital category hero's globe, network dots and screen.
- Watch for outline-only elements — every major mass must carry the teal fill; a candidate
  whose padlock body, envelope, or page renders as bare linework fails the 40px test.
- White background is intentional — the primary sits on a neutral tile; the frame
  comes from the tile, not the art.
- After generating: save as `static/img/topics/encryption-vs-lawful-access.png`,
  set `Topic.primary_art = "img/topics/encryption-vs-lawful-access.png"`, and
  regenerate the social PNGs
  (`render_social --slug encryption-vs-lawful-access --formats og,sq,pt`).
