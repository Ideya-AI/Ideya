# Audit: Immigration & Borders
Date: 2026-09-23. Verdict: ready.

The page tells one story. Basics sets the scale (stock, flows, displacement, opinion) and then the recent fall in crossings and net migration, and the why-now (the EU Pact taking effect in June 2026) belongs to that fall. The eight questions each give two sides at close to the same length and with their own evidence. The five camps are named by what they want, run 211 to 248 words each, and are distinct: cut, the control-plus-legal-routes bargain, expansion, asylum rights and free movement. The crux draws both of its halves from the questions above it: the empirical half from wages, budgets, housing and deterrence, the values half from membership, asylum and the right to move. Its closing line, that either side can accept every finding and keep its view, is even-handed. The figures agree with each other wherever they meet (Gallup 55 to 29 or 30, UK 813,000 minus 642,000 = 171,000, Frontex 26 and 38 percent drops, Cato’s 44 and 75 percent). The renders are newer than topic.json, and the live page at 1440 matches them. Nothing here would mislead a reader. The notes below are judgment calls and small repeats for the human.

## Notes for the human
- Crux lead: “How much do residents lose when newcomers arrive” can read as if residents lose on net, and the Expand camp argues the opposite (“make the countries that receive them richer”). The subtitle limits the agreed point to “some residents bear real costs,” so the reading holds. Something like “What do residents gain or lose…” would be more neutral. The same wording appears on all three share cards.
- Overview summary and question 8 say “peaceful people,” which is the framing the free-movement side uses. It does mark out the hard case (no one argues for admitting people who pose a threat), so I left it as a judgment call.
- The incarceration stat uses “Illegal immigrants” in its chart labels and note, which is the source’s (Cato’s) term. Elsewhere the page says “immigrants in the country illegally.” Both sides find some of these terms loaded, so it is worth a look.
- Incarceration stat detail against question 5: the stat says critics (CIS) prefer Texas arrest records, and question 5 says Texas data show lower immigrant rates. Both are true of the real dispute, but a careful reader may pause here.
- Protect-asylum detail: “after an August 2025 ruling of the EU Court of Justice, Italy’s centers in Albania were repurposed.” My memory says the repurposing came in spring 2025, before the ruling. Italy’s March 2025 decree and the CJEU judgment of August 1, 2025 would settle the order.
- Protect-asylum detail ends with “UNHCR itself engages with governments on safeguards rather than opposing return hubs outright,” which concedes a point inside the camp’s own case. The Close-irregular-routes camp makes the same UNHCR point, so a reader meets it twice.
- Repeats a reader will meet twice:
  - the world’s 3.7 percent migrant share (the World bar in stat 1 and all of stat 2)
  - “deaths exceed births from 2030” (labor-force stat detail and the Expand camp)
  - Gallup 55 to about 30 (stat 6 and the Expand camp)
  - two-thirds of refugees living in neighboring countries (displacement stat and the asylum camp)
- The detention stat’s snapshot is from July 11, 2026, but its source was accessed on September 23. If TRAC has published a newer count since then, the page shows an old one.
- Actions: Protect-asylum has two linked actions (Amnesty and IRC) and every other camp has one. IRC’s volunteer work helps new arrivals of any kind and could go untagged. “Contact your elected representatives” is US-only on a page that also covers the EU and UK.
- Template/CSS at 1440, seen on the live page too:
  - In three-column Basics rows, the units “applications” and “detections” wrap onto their own indented line under 822,000 and 178,000.
  - The last Basics row holds two tiles and leaves the third slot empty.
- Template/CSS at 390: the ranked-chart bars are very short (about 40px of track), so gaps like 74 against 31 flatten out on mobile.
- Share cards list four of the five camps (free movement is dropped by the cap), the same as the collapsed Positions section. This is by design, noted for awareness.
