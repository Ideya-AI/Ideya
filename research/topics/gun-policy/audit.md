## Notes for the human
- Question 2 (AR-15), the case for protecting them: "about 3 percent of gun murders where the weapon is known" misreads the weapons tile. That tile gives 3 percent of all FBI-reported gun murders, with the weapon unknown in 42 percent, so the share among cases where the weapon is known is about 5 percent. The point (a small share) still stands. Fix: "about 3 percent of gun murders in FBI data".
- The mass-shootings tile note says the FBI count "found 23" without a unit. The detail then gives 24 active-shooter incidents in 2024, so a reader cannot tell whether 23 is deaths or a second, conflicting incident count. Name the unit.
- Firearm-homicide tile: Brazil shows 5.8 per 100k, only about 30 percent above the US's 4.4, yet the detail calls Mexico, Colombia and Brazil "far higher". Brazil's figure also looks low next to its commonly reported gun homicide levels. The Our World in Data grapher cited on the tile would settle it. If 5.8 is right, soften "far higher" for Brazil.
- The fall in gun homicide since 2021 is used five times: the suicides-and-homicides tile, question 1's not-the-driver case, question 3's deters-crime case, the Concentrate position, and the safety-opinion tile. Also "about six times Canada's" (firearm-homicide tile and question 1) and nine-in-ten gun suicide lethality (lethality tile and question 1). A reader meets each twice or more. None of this misleads.
- The US Department of Justice is listed as a proponent of Protect and expand, while the Concentrate position places "the Trump administration" in its enforcement wing. The same actor shows up in two camps. That is defensible, but worth a look: should a government department be named as a camp member at all?
- Question 4 (checks and licensing) is tagged empirical, but its wording ("enough to justify the burden") and lean labels ("Worth the burden / Not worth it") are value judgments. Consider re-tagging it or rewording it to the empirical half.
- Layout, 1440 expanded Basics: the last five tiles (Gun death rate by state through Australia) are all wide, so each sits alone in a row with the right third of the grid empty. This is content-caused: there are no narrow tiles left to pair after the child-deaths tile. Interleaving the narrow tiles differently, or accepting the pattern, is the editor's or your call.
- Art: a bolt-action rifle on a rest, aimed down a winding path at a target. It reads as sport shooting rather than as any camp's argument. Acceptable, but it shows only the owner's side of the subject; worth a glance.
- views.md reports Basics as "all 10 tiles"; the page has 18 tiles, and the expanded renders show all 18.
