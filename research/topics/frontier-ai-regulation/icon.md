# Regulating Frontier AI — primary icon art brief

Handoff brief for an illustrator or image/vector tool. Full style theme: `../ICONOGRAPHY.md`.
Template siblings: `nuclear-energy.md` (the tuned reference),
`birth-rate-decline-pronatalism.md` (most recent), `ai-takeoff-loss-of-control.md`
and `ai-future-of-work.md` (the other AI-category scenes — this one must not echo them).

## Use
The topic's **primary hero image**. Shows large on the topic page, poster, and social
cards; also reduced into a rounded-square tinted tile and shown on a dark band. Must
read both large and small, on light and dark.

## Style (non-negotiable)
- **Minimal monoline** — ONE consistent stroke weight across the whole image, rounded
  caps/joins. References: **Gorges Beer Co** menu icons, **Flaticon "Lineal."**
- **Duotone, single color, two tones** — line at full strength + a soft (~16–18%) fill
  of the **same hue** on the masses. No second color, no gradients, no shadows.
- **Color:** the category color (AI & Machine Intelligence = **tech blue**,
  `#3b7ea1`). Deliver single-color so it can be recolored per category.
- Flat, clean, editorial.
- **Filled masses, not linework-only** — lesson from earlier topics: thin unfilled
  linework nearly vanished at 40px. **Every** major element in this scene is a filled
  shape carrying the duotone tint; nothing important may be a bare line.

## Composition
A small bespoke **scene** combining several *simple* sub-icons in one frame — about
**2.5–3.5× the complexity of a single Gorges icon**. Generate wide (3:2); framing is
normalized in post (trim to content, ~8% pad, fit 3:2 on white). Legible from ~40px
to hero size, readable on a dark background.

**Distinct from the other AI-category scenes.** `ai-takeoff-loss-of-control` is a
trajectory scene (two rising curves, a rocket, a human figure at a lever);
`ai-future-of-work` is a two-party scene (a shared worktable with a person on one side
and a machine arm on the other). This one is an **object-only still life** — no human
figure, no hand, no machine arm, no curves, no table, no arrows — three objects
standing apart on one ground line, with the space between them deliberately empty.
That emptiness *is* the topic: the rule that would connect the instrument to the thing
has not been written yet.

## Elements — promise *and* peril
The debate is how strictly to govern the most capable systems, whether rules bite at
the *use*, the *model*, or the *training run*, and **who holds the pen** — a statehouse,
a capital, or a treaty. Nine camps run from deregulate through horizontal law,
model-level mandates, sectoral enforcement and international coordination to a pause.
The art must hold the *question* open — it must not say AI is dangerous, must not say
regulation is strangling anything, and must not pick a venue:
- **A gavel lying level and at rest on its round sounding block** — the focal point on
  one side, echoing the topic's Phosphor `gavel` glyph: the rule-making instrument.
  Lying **flat and still on** the block, handle horizontal, **never raised, never
  mid-swing, never striking anything**, with no impact marks, motion lines, or radiating
  arcs. A gavel that has come down has rendered a verdict; this one has not been picked
  up. Filled mass.
- **A plain processor chip standing on the same ground line** — the counterweight on
  the other side: the frontier model, the thing being governed. A simple square chip
  body with a few short, even pins along two edges; **whole, plain, and intact** — no
  crack, no glow, no rays, no face, no eye, no brain shape inside, no warning mark, and
  nothing enclosing it. Equal in visual weight and height to the gavel-and-block, so
  neither object dominates or sits above the other. Filled mass.
- **A single blank sheet of paper lying flat on the ground between them** — the rule
  itself, unwritten. Completely blank: **no lines of text, no ruling, no seal, no
  signature, no emblem**. It lies in the open middle of the scene and **touches
  neither** the gavel nor the chip — the law has not yet reached either one. Filled mass.
- **A plain pen resting on the blank sheet, unheld** — "who holds the pen," left
  unanswered: the pen is simply set down across the page, **no hand, no arm, no figure
  anywhere in the image**, nib not touching any mark. Filled mass.
- **A single straight horizontal ground line across the scene** — one plane under the
  sounding block, the sheet, and the chip alike (mirrors the sibling compositions):
  the instrument and the governed thing stand on the same ground, at the same level.

The neutrality is structural: two objects of equal weight, an unwritten page between
them, and no arrow, tether, bracket, enclosure, or connecting line of any kind joining
them. Nothing in the frame states which way the relationship resolves.

## Mood
Neutral and editorial — an instrument set down, a page still blank, a chip standing
whole, all on one level plane. Where the rule should bite, how far upstream it should
reach, and whose hand picks up the pen are the open questions the scene holds. Not a
danger scene, not a crackdown, not a celebration of an unregulated frontier.

## Avoid
Photorealism; more than one color; gradients/shadows; **a raised, swinging, or
striking gavel**, a gavel hitting the chip or the sheet, impact bursts, motion arcs, or
shock lines (verdict: the rule has come down); warning triangles, exclamation marks,
hazard stripes, red zones, skulls, mushroom clouds, a cracked or broken or sparking
chip, a containment breach (verdict: AI is dangerous); chains, padlocks, cages, bars,
fences, nets, boxes, brackets, or any enclosure around the chip, and any leash, tether,
or cord attached to it (verdict: AI must be shackled); red tape, ribbon, or cords
wrapped around, tangling, or choking anything, a stamp crushing a spark, a rocket or
lightbulb being blocked, weighed down, or held back, a wilting or extinguished flame
(verdict: regulation strangles innovation); any national flag, flag colors, EU stars,
a Capitol or parliament dome, a UN emblem or laurel, a government seal, a map, or any
country outline (verdict: this venue writes the rule); anthropomorphic or humanoid
robots, robot heads or hands, glowing brains, neural-net "mind" motifs, or a single
watching eye; any human figure, hand, arm, or silhouette at all; balance scales,
courtrooms, judges' benches, witness stands, or law-book stacks with spines; arrows,
chevrons, dashed trajectories, up/down charts, gauges, dials, or countdown clocks;
fussy internal detail (circuit-trace mazes or die patterns on the chip, wood grain on
the gavel, ruled lines or watermark on the sheet, a clip or engraving on the pen);
clutter; anything that reads as pro- or anti-regulation, or pro- or anti-AI.

## Deliverable
Ideally **vector (SVG)**, single-color, transparent ground. PNG accepted
(the pipeline is raster-primary).

## Generation prompt (Nano Banana / Gemini)
**Frameless** — the art has NO drawn border. The CSS tile renders the rounded-square
frame. Lessons carried over from the earlier generations: force the soft fill to
~15–18% (it renders near-invisible if unstressed), force uniform stroke weight (thin
secondary elements drift thinner), and make every major element a **filled mass** —
earlier linework-only elements nearly vanished at 40px.

**Fresh-generation prompt:**
> A minimalist flat vector-style icon illustration of a rule-making scene, in a clean editorial line-art style. Combine several simple objects into one small, balanced composition, all of them standing on a single thin straight horizontal ground line that runs across the whole scene: on the left, a simple gavel lying flat and level ON TOP OF its round sounding block, handle horizontal and head resting still — the gavel is AT REST and NOT raised, NOT tilted up, NOT mid-swing, NOT striking anything, with no impact lines, no motion arcs and no radiating marks anywhere; on the right, a plain simple computer processor chip standing on the same ground line — a square chip body with a few short even pins along two edges, completely whole and plain, with no crack, no glow, no rays, no face, no eye and nothing drawn inside it; and in the open space between them, lying flat on the ground, a single completely blank rectangular sheet of paper with a plain simple pen resting across it — the sheet is entirely empty with no writing, no ruled lines, no seal and no signature, and the pen is simply lying down on the paper by itself. There is NO hand, NO arm, NO person, NO figure and NO robot anywhere in the image, and nothing connects the gavel to the chip — no arrow, no line, no rope, no chain, no bracket, no box and no enclosure of any kind.
>
> Important: NO border, NO frame, NO box or outline around the scene — just the illustration itself on a plain background. Keep the composition compact and centered, not spread wide, with the gavel-and-block on one side and the chip on the other at EQUAL visual weight and equal height — neither larger, taller, higher, nor more dominant than the other, and neither placed above the other — and keep a clear empty gap between them so the blank sheet and pen sit alone in the middle without touching either object.
>
> Style: monoline line art with ONE uniform stroke weight on every element — the gavel and its handle, the round sounding block, the chip body and its pins, the sheet of paper, the pen, and the ground line must all be exactly the same thickness. Rounded line caps and corners. Confident, slightly bold strokes so it stays legible when small, about 2.5 to 3.5 times the complexity of one simple glyph — each object stays simple, the richness is the combination. No fussy internal detail — no circuit traces or die pattern inside the chip, no wood grain or engraving on the gavel, no ruled lines or watermark on the paper, no clip or nib detail on the pen.
>
> Color: a single blue hue, two tones only. Draw all lines in solid blue (#3b7ea1), and give EVERY major element a clearly visible soft fill of the same blue at about 16-18% opacity — fill the gavel head and handle, the round sounding block, the chip body, the sheet of paper, and the pen with a light blue tint so the two-tone duotone is obviously present rather than white interiors; no major element may be left as unfilled outline-only linework. No other colors, no gradients, no shadows, no offset or misregistered fills, no 3D, no texture.
>
> Composition: wide 3:2 aspect, centered with generous, even padding; balanced optical weight — the level ground line runs along the bottom, the resting gavel anchors one side, the whole chip the other, the blank sheet and pen lie in the middle. Plain white background. Calm, neutral, editorial mood — an instrument set down and a page not yet written, with the relationship between the rule and the machine left an open question; not a crackdown, not a warning, not a celebration. No raised or striking gavel, no warning triangles, no hazard signs, no cracked or broken chip, no chains, padlocks, cages, bars, fences or nets, no red tape or cords wrapped around anything, no rocket or lightbulb, no flags, no domes, no government seals, no maps, no globes, no balance scales, no robots, no brains, no eyes, no arrows or charts. No text, letters, words, numbers or symbols anywhere in the image, including on the sheet of paper and on the chip.

**Edit-mode instruction (after the first generation, feed the concept back in):**
> Using this image, keep the same composition, elements, and layout, but: (1) remove any border or frame around the scene entirely; (2) add a clearly visible soft light-blue fill (~16% opacity of the same hue) inside EVERY major element — the gavel head and handle, the round sounding block, the chip body, the sheet of paper, and the pen — so it reads as a two-tone duotone with no outline-only elements left; (3) make every line exactly the same thickness, matching the main outline weight — including the chip's pins, the pen, the edges of the sheet, and the ground line; (4) lay the gavel completely flat and level on top of its round sounding block, at rest — lower it out of any raised or angled striking pose and delete any impact lines, motion arcs, or radiating marks around it; (5) keep the sheet of paper completely blank and the pen simply resting on it with no hand, arm or figure touching it, and remove any writing, ruled lines, seal, signature or emblem from the page; (6) keep the chip whole and plain — remove any crack, glow, rays, face, eye, or inner pattern — and remove anything enclosing, chaining or connecting it to the gavel; (7) keep the gavel-and-block and the chip at equal size and equal height on the same ground line, neither above the other. Keep it flat, single blue hue, no gradients, shadows, extra colors, or text.

**Notes:**
- Over-generate on the **resting gavel** and the **blank sheet with the unheld pen**;
  those are the two elements most likely to come out wrong. The generator badly wants
  to raise the gavel into a striking pose, tilt it, add impact bursts or motion arcs,
  or swing it down onto the chip — any of which renders the verdict "the rule has
  come down on AI" and disqualifies the candidate. It equally wants to fill the page
  with lines of writing, add a seal, signature or heading, or attach a hand holding the
  pen — writing on the page answers the question the scene exists to hold open, and a
  hand answers "who holds the pen."
- Watch for the generator editorializing in either direction — a warning triangle, a
  cracked or sparking chip, a padlock, chain, cage or bracket around the chip (verdict:
  AI is dangerous / must be shackled), or red tape, cords, a stamp, or a blocked rocket
  or lightbulb (verdict: regulation strangles innovation). Both mirror failures
  disqualify.
- Watch for venue symbols sneaking onto the page or into the background — a flag, EU
  stars, a domed capitol, a laurel wreath, a globe, or a government seal. The scene must
  name no venue; the unheld pen is the only statement about who writes the rule.
- Watch for the chip drifting into a brain, a robot head, or a glowing eye, and for a
  human figure or hand appearing anywhere — this scene has no people in it at all,
  which is also what keeps it visually distinct from the other two AI topics.
- Watch for outline-only elements — every major mass must carry the blue fill; a
  candidate whose sheet, pen, or chip renders as bare linework fails the 40px test.
- White background is intentional — the primary sits on a neutral tile; the frame
  comes from the tile, not the art.
- After generating: save as `static/img/topics/frontier-ai-regulation.png`,
  set `Topic.primary_art = "img/topics/frontier-ai-regulation.png"`, and
  regenerate the social PNGs
  (`render_social --slug frontier-ai-regulation --formats og,sq,pt`).

## Selection

**Winner: `gemini-3-pro-image.4.jpg`** (2026-08-13, first generation round). All four
candidates produced the gavel / blank-page / chip still life with no human figure, no
warning marks, no cage or tether, and no venue emblem — the neutrality core held across
the board. Scoring against the brief:

- **#1** — the sheet renders as a tall upright rectangle rather than lying flat, so it
  reads as a blank board or screen rather than an unwritten rule; pen mid-write against
  it. Fail.
- **#2** — cleanest object separation and the only one where the pen rests beside the
  page rather than on it, but the sheet is **outline-only** (no duotone fill) and the
  sheet-and-pen pair floats below the ground line, detached from the plane the gavel and
  chip stand on. Runner-up.
- **#3** — same unfilled sheet below the ground line, and the gavel head reads slightly
  more raised. Fail.
- **#4** — every major mass filled (gavel head, sounding block, sheet, chip), all three
  objects on the single ground line with the page lying flat in the open middle, page
  entirely blank, chip whole and unmarked, no hand anywhere, uniform stroke weight
  including the chip pins. **Winner.**

Accepted deviations: the gavel head rests upright on its block rather than lying fully
level — it reads as set down, not mid-swing, so the "verdict already rendered" failure
the brief guards against does not occur. The pen's tip rests against the blank page
rather than clear of it; since no written mark is drawn, the page still reads unwritten.
Fills again render nearer ~35% opacity than the specced 16–18%, consistent with every
prior topic's accepted winner.
