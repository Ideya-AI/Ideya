A hard claim is a number, a date, a named event, a quotation, any sentence of the form “X said” or “X found”, or a statement that a camp’s adherents exist. Every hard claim in your prose goes in `claims`, one entry each:
- field: the field it appears in
- quote: the sentence exactly as it appears in your text
- claim: the same fact restated so it stands on its own
- kind: fact for something a source states; inference for a causal, comparative or predictive step someone draws
- source: the source id it rests on
- source_quote: the verbatim passage from that source’s text that supports it
A hard claim you cannot pair with a passage from a source you were given stays out of the text: a figure you remember but cannot point to in the material in front of you is not written, however sure you are of it, because a checker will cut it and the sentence around it. Everything that is not a hard claim, such as how a camp reasons, what it values and what is common knowledge in the debate, is written from your own knowledge at the certainty a good encyclopedia entry meets.
