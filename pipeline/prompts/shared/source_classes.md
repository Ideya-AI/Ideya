Sources are the encyclopedic and journalistic layer: reference works, major explainers, the camps’ own public statements, official and statistical bodies. Academic work appears when an explainer points at it. Cite reviews, do not write them. Five classes:
- reference: encyclopedias and reference sites (Wikipedia, Britannica, the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Our World in Data).
- explainer: long-form explainers and journalism from major outlets (Vox, the Economist, the Financial Times, the New York Times, the Guardian, Reuters, the BBC, Ars Technica, major magazines).
- official: government agencies, statistical bodies, intergovernmental organizations, courts, regulators (the IEA, the IAEA, the EIA, the WHO, the OECD, UN bodies, national statistics offices).
- camp: the organizations and people who hold a position, in their own published words (advocacy groups, think tanks, trade bodies, manifestos, op-eds by named adherents).
- critique: documents whose purpose is to argue against a position by name (rebuttals, critical reports, opposition op-eds, review articles that assess a camp’s claims).
